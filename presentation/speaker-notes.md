# Bizroam — 4-minute pitch

English deck · 5 slides · target: 3–5 minutes.

## 1. The need — 40 seconds

“We’re two French people visiting San Francisco. We know this city is full of startups and major tech companies, but walking down a street doesn’t tell us who is inside, what they build, or how big they are. We kept asking the same questions: who’s here, and which companies would actually interest us? So we built Bizroam: a personal AI guide to the companies around you.”

## 2. The solution — 50 seconds

“You choose your interests with tags or free text. We start with tech startups and major tech companies, because that’s what we’re curious about. Then you share your location and start walking. The map follows you and discovers places in a 500-meter radius. The agent researches relevant companies while the map stays responsive. Names make the map readable, and company-value circles give a sense of scale. Those values have a source and a date; an unknown value is N/A.”

## 3. The experience — 55 seconds

“A discovery becomes a profile: what the company does, what it builds, why it matches your interests, and sources you can check. We verify the address where possible and label uncertain locations. This is Nylas from our actual SF session, with research sources and public Hacker News discussions. You don’t have to watch the screen continuously: passing within a hundred meters saves a place to your trail. The history and preferences persist in your browser, without creating an account. Discovery alerts stay inside the app.”

## 4. The agent and sponsors — 60 seconds

“Each walk has a persistent Codex conversation. It gets location and interests, discovers candidates, selects relevant companies, verifies them, and publishes profiles. The tool access is narrow, with cached research and bounded budgets. Cline is part of the development workflow. Parallel’s Responses API creates structured company research with citations and address evidence. Composio connects a restricted Hacker News search tool, so we can add public tech-community discussions to the profile. OpenStreetMap provides the map and nearby-place geography.”

## 5. Proof and close — 30 seconds

“These are real numbers from one saved San Francisco walk: thirty-five places discovered, five sourced company profiles, and five places in the passed-by trail. The screenshots use that actual saved session. The app is deployed as our password-protected personal WIP. Bizroam turns an unfamiliar street into a useful discovery. Stay curious—there’s a company around the corner.”

Total target: 3 minutes 55 seconds. Leave a little time for the live demo or questions.
